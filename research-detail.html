The 150-day target: The government's Life Sciences Sector Plan commits to reducing clinical trial setup time to under 150 days by March 2026 — from regulatory submission to first participant recruited. The 150-day clock comprises three stages: MHRA/HRA approval (target: 60 days), site opening after approval (target: 60 days), and first participant recruited after site opening (target: 30 days). The data below relates to the site-level component: whether studies recruited their first participant within 90 days of HRA approval. This is a key NIHR Research Delivery Network performance metric, with funding implications for trusts.

Site Set-Up Performance — UKCRD Monthly Tracking

Primary tracking series for this page. Sourced directly from the UK Clinical Research Delivery (UKCRD) programme's Trust Level Set-Up Report (ukcrd.org), drawing on the NIHR Research Delivery Network's Central Portfolio Management System (CPMS). Tracks commercial interventional studies recruiting their first participant within 90 days of HRA approval or site selection, excluding extension, rare disease and low-recruiting studies. Republished monthly on a rolling 6-month window. The Shelford aggregate below adds up the studies across all 10 trusts and works out what share hit each milestone — not an average of the ten trusts' separate percentages, which would count a 3-study trust the same as a 25-study trust. UKCRD only publishes rounded percentages per trust, not the underlying study counts, so we back-calculated each trust's count from its percentage and total studies before adding them up.

Source: UKCRD Trust Level Set-Up Report, ukcrd.org (NIHR RDN CPMS data). Four consecutive monthly snapshots — report dates 03 Jun, 03 Jul, 04 Aug and 03 Sep 2026, each a rolling 6-month window. An earlier snapshot (30 Apr 2026, cut 25 Apr 2026, window since 07 Apr 2025) used the pre-format-change methodology and is shown separately below as a baseline, not part of the trend. No May 2026 snapshot exists on the archive — the site jumps from the 25 Apr cut straight to the 01 Jun cut.

Why the 60-day and 30-day rates can be higher than the 90-day rate: the 90-day figure measures the full elapsed time from approval to first participant. The 60-day and 30-day figures each measure only one segment of that journey (approval → site confirmed; and site ready → first participant) and explicitly exclude the stage in between (site confirmed → site ready, e.g. contracting), which UKCRD treats as outside the site's control but which still counts toward the 90-day clock. A study can clear both segment milestones and still miss the overall 90-day target if enough time is lost in that excluded middle stage — so the three percentages aren't nested and shouldn't be expected to move together.
